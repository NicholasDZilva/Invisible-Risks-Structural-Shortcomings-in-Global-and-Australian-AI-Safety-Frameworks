# Invisible Risks: Structural Shortcomings in Global and Australian AI Safety Frameworks

**Author:** Nicholas D’Zilva  
**Date:** March 2026  

This repository contains the paper **Invisible Risks: Structural Shortcomings in Global and Australian AI Safety Frameworks**, which examines gaps in current AI safety standards and proposes structural oversight mechanisms to strengthen democracy, governance, and sovereign AI capabilities.  

The paper is intended for **government departments, policy teams, researchers, and technologists** interested in AI ethics, AI governance, and sovereign infrastructure planning, both globally and in Australia.  

---

## Abstract

AI safety frameworks are proliferating globally, yet they remain largely output-focused and compliance-driven, leaving systemic blind spots unaddressed. Hidden structural risks — such as authority concentration, suppressed dissent, and irreversible decisions — threaten both democratic legitimacy and sovereign governance. This paper highlights these shortcomings and examines their implications for Australia, proposing the adoption of structural oversight mechanisms to complement current standards and strengthen resilience in national and global AI systems.
